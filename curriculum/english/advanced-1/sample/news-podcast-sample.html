Students Build Solar Pump for Village

Read a real-style news article, and find the five key facts of the story.

Read a news articleFind who, what, where, when, and whyUnderstand: It is said that… / is expected to…

1Read the article

The Daily NileThursday, May 7

Students Build Solar Pump That Brings Water to a Village

A team of young engineers has given a village near Kassala clean water every day, using only the power of the sun.

By our science reporter

A village near Kassala has had clean water every day this month, thanks to a solar-powered water pump that was designed and built by a team of five engineering students. The pump is believed to be the first of its kind in the area.

Before the pump was installed, women and children in the village had been walking up to two hours a day to bring water from a well. "It used to take my whole morning," said one mother. "Now my children go to school on time."

“Now my children go to school on time.”A mother in the village

The pump is powered by six solar panels, and it is said to bring up more than ten thousand liters of water a day from deep under the ground. The water is stored in a large tank and cleaned before it is used. It is reported that the whole system cost less than a small car.

The team was led by Sara Osman, a second-year student. "We had been studying solar energy in class," she said, "but we wanted to see it change real lives." The students are expected to build two more pumps in nearby villages next year, and other universities are said to be interested in the design.

Engineers have welcomed the project. It is widely agreed that small, cheap solar systems could bring clean water to thousands of villages across Sudan.
