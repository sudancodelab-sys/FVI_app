Who Ate the Cake?

A birthday cake disappears from the institute, and everyone is a suspect. Read the story, then read it from Mr. Ali's point of view.

Report what people said with reporting verbsUse the right pattern after each verbTell a funny mystery

1The story

It was almost Mr. Ali's sixtieth birthday, and his students had planned a surprise. On Wednesday afternoon, Huda bought a big chocolate cake from the best bakery in town and hid it in the fridge in the teachers' room. The party was going to be on Thursday morning, before the first class, and everyone promised to keep it a secret.

On Thursday, Huda opened the fridge. The cake was gone. "Somebody has taken it!" she cried. Within minutes, the whole class was in the teachers' room, and everyone was looking at everyone else.

Omar looked at Adam. "You were here late last night," he said. Adam denied going anywhere near the fridge. "I was only printing the birthday card," he said. Then Sara accused Omar of eating it, because there was chocolate on his shirt. "You've been caught red-handed!" she said. Omar admitted eating chocolate, but only a chocolate bar from the shop. "I refuse to be blamed for a crime I didn't commit," he said dramatically.

Huda was close to tears. Sara, practical as always, suggested checking the security camera in the corridor. The guard agreed to show them the video, but he warned them not to expect much. "That camera is older than I am," he said.

They watched in silence. At 9:40 the night before, a figure walked into the teachers' room and came out carrying a large white box. The picture wasn't clear, but the walk was. Everyone knew that slow, careful walk. The thief turned out to be Mr. Ali himself.

At that moment, the door opened, and Mr. Ali came in, carrying the box. "Good morning," he said. "I found this in the fridge last night. There was no name on it, and the cleaners empty the fridge on Thursdays, so I took it home to keep it safe." Then he opened the box and read the words on top: Happy Birthday, Mr. Ali. He laughed so much that he had to sit down.

He apologized for spoiling the surprise, and the students insisted on having the party anyway. "Well," said Mr. Ali, "don't judge a book by its cover. I looked like a thief, and so did Omar." As for Omar, everyone agreed that he still owed them a chocolate bar.

3Point of view Now Mr. Ali tells it.

MA

On Wednesday night, I stayed late to mark exams. Before I left, I opened the fridge and found a big white box with no name on it. The cleaners empty the fridge on Thursdays, so I decided to take it home and keep it safe. I didn't even look inside.

The next morning, I walked into the teachers' room with the box and found my whole class there. They looked at me as if I were a criminal, and poor Huda looked as if she had been crying.

Then I opened the box and read the words on the cake. I laughed until I had to sit down. I apologized for spoiling their surprise, and I promised to look inside every box before I take it home.

Later, Sara told me the whole story. Adam had denied going near the fridge, and Omar had been accused of eating the cake. Omar still insists on telling everyone that he is innocent. I believe him. Mostly.

4What did you learn?

You learned reporting verbs.

When we report what people said, we often choose a verb that shows what they meant, a promise, a warning, or an apology. Each verb has its own pattern.

PatternExample
to do · promise, agree, refuse, offerEveryone promised to keep it a secret.
someone (not) to do · warn, advise, ask, tellHe warned them not to expect much.
-ing · deny, admit, suggestAdam denied going near the fridge.
someone of / for -ing · accuse, blameSara accused Omar of eating it.
for / on -ing · apologize for, insist onHe apologized for spoiling the surprise.
suggest + -ingSara suggested checking the camera. Not: suggested to check.
Negative: not before to or -ingHe warned them not to expect much. He admitted not knowing.
They say more than said"It wasn't me!" He denied doing it. "I'll help." He offered to help.
QuestionsWhat have you promised to do this week?I've promised to help my brother with his homework.

5Idioms, proverbs and phrases

idiombe caught red-handed

to be found while you are doing something wrong

The boy was caught red-handed with his hand in the cookie jar.
phraseturn out

to be found to be true in the end, often as a surprise

The exam turned out to be easier than we expected.
proverbDon't judge a book by its cover.

Don't decide what someone or something is like only from the way they look.

The restaurant looked old, but the food was wonderful. Don't judge a book by its cover.
